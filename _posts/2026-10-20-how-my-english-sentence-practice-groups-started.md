## It started with a simple idea

I have always believed that improving spoken English requires more than memorising vocabulary or studying grammar. It requires regular practice, careful listening, and feedback.

Many English learners spend a lot of time studying English. They learn new words, read articles, watch videos, and practise grammar exercises. However, when it comes to speaking, they may still feel unsure about how to pronounce certain sounds, where to place stress, or how to connect words naturally.

I noticed that one of the biggest challenges was not a lack of learning materials. It was the lack of consistent speaking practice and individual feedback.

That was where my idea came from: what if learners could practise a little every day and receive personal feedback on their pronunciation?

I decided to turn this idea into an English pronunciation practice group.

## From individual practice to a daily routine

I wanted to make English speaking practice simple enough to become part of people's daily lives.

The routine is straightforward. Every weekday morning, I share an English word and a sentence in the group. Members practise and record their voices whenever they have time. I then listen to their recordings and provide individual audio feedback.

The feedback is not simply about whether a pronunciation is right or wrong. I pay attention to specific sounds, word stress, sentence stress, linking, rhythm, and other details that affect natural speech.

Sometimes I listen to the same recording several times to identify exactly what needs improvement. I may record my own voice to demonstrate a sound or explain how to adjust the pronunciation.

The next day, members receive their feedback and move on to a new practice task.

Over time, this simple routine has become a regular part of my day.

## Why I created two groups

As the practice developed, I realised that learners had different levels and different needs.

Some were still working on individual sounds and basic sentence pronunciation. Others could pronounce most words reasonably well but wanted to improve their fluency and sound more natural when speaking longer sentences.

So I created two groups.

**Words and Short Sentences**

This group is designed for learners who want to build a stronger pronunciation foundation. The daily practice focuses on individual words and short sentences. It helps learners become more familiar with English sounds and develop greater confidence in speaking.

**Longer Sentence Practice**

This group is for learners who already have some pronunciation foundation and want to work on connected speech. Longer sentences provide more opportunities to practise word stress, sentence stress, linking, rhythm, and intonation.

I believe that practising individual words is important, but it is not enough. Speaking naturally also means knowing how sounds work together in a complete sentence.

## Why I focus on pronunciation

My interest in pronunciation comes from a broader belief about communication: speaking is not just about producing words. It is about making yourself understood.

Having an accent is completely normal. People come from different linguistic backgrounds, and there is no need to sound exactly like a native speaker.

However, some pronunciation problems can affect communication. For example, if a particular sound is unclear, a listener may understand a different word. Even when individual words are understandable, unnatural stress or unclear linking can make a sentence difficult to follow.

I have also noticed that pronunciation practice can improve listening comprehension. When learners understand how sounds change in connected speech, they become better at recognising those patterns when listening to other people.

For me, the goal is not to eliminate someone's accent. It is to help them speak more clearly, confidently, and naturally.

## Why I personally listen to every recording

One thing that makes these groups different is that I listen to members' recordings myself and provide individual feedback.

This takes time. My afternoons are often occupied with speaking lessons, and my mornings are dedicated to French classes. Around lunchtime, I usually put on my headphones and work through the voice messages from the previous day.

I listen carefully, identify pronunciation issues, and record feedback for each learner. Sometimes I replay a recording several times because I want to make sure that my suggestions address the actual problem.

I could simply share pronunciation materials and ask everyone to practise independently. But I believe feedback is an essential part of learning.

A learner may not notice their own pronunciation habits. They may repeat the same mistake many times without realising it. A specific explanation or demonstration can help them understand what to change and how to practise.

I also want members to learn from one another. When they listen to feedback given to other members, they may recognise similar problems in their own speech. This creates opportunities to learn beyond their individual recordings.

## What keeps me motivated

What motivates me is seeing people make progress through consistent practice.

Pronunciation improvement does not usually happen overnight. It comes from noticing small differences, correcting repeated mistakes, and gradually developing better speaking habits.

I enjoy helping learners understand why a sound is not quite right and showing them how to improve it. I also appreciate the fact that everyone has different challenges. Some people struggle with particular consonants, while others need to work on stress, rhythm, or connected speech.

Every recording gives me an opportunity to understand a learner's needs a little better.

I also believe that learning should be practical and sustainable. Instead of asking people to spend hours practising every day, I want to make it possible to practise a small amount regularly.

A word, a sentence, and a few minutes of focused practice may not seem like much. But repeated consistently, these small efforts can make a meaningful difference.

## A small practice, repeated every day

The groups started with a simple idea: make pronunciation practice more consistent, personal, and practical.

Today, the routine remains much the same. A daily prompt, a voice recording, and individual feedback.

There is no shortcut to improving spoken English. But with the right practice and regular feedback, learners can become more aware of their pronunciation and gradually communicate with greater confidence.

That is why I continue doing this every day.
