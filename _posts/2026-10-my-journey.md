# My Journey: From Software Engineering to English Teaching

*Different experiences, one common thread: communication.*

People sometimes ask me how I became an English teacher. My journey was not a straight line. Before teaching English, I spent years working in the software industry, supporting professional teams, improving business processes, and communicating across languages and cultures.

Today, I am a PhD student in Software Engineering in Montreal, Canada, and I also teach English part-time. These experiences may seem quite different, but they have shaped the way I approach teaching and learning.

## 2013: My First Step into the Software Industry

My professional journey began in 2013 when I joined a Chinese software company as a Quality Assurance Engineer.

Working in software quality assurance taught me to pay attention to details, think systematically, identify problems, and understand how different parts of a project work together.

I gradually developed an interest not only in software products themselves, but also in the processes and people behind them.

This experience gave me a solid foundation in professional communication, analytical thinking, and problem-solving.

## 2016: Moving into Software Consulting

In 2016, I moved into software consulting, focusing on software process improvement.

My work involved helping organizations understand their existing processes, identify areas for improvement, and develop more effective ways of working.

An important part of this experience was supporting international assessment projects. I sometimes worked as an interpreter for English-speaking Lead Appraisers, helping facilitate communication between international experts and Chinese teams.

This was a particularly valuable experience for me.

I realized that communication is much more than translating words from one language into another. It is about understanding meaning, explaining ideas clearly, asking the right questions, and making sure people from different backgrounds understand each other.

Over the years, I accumulated approximately eight years of experience in the software industry.

## 2024: A New Chapter in Montreal

In 2024, I began a PhD in Software Engineering in Montreal, Canada.

Starting a new academic chapter in another country brought new experiences and challenges. It also gave me opportunities to work in a multicultural environment and continue developing my academic and professional communication skills.

My research focuses on software measurement and estimation, particularly how software size and project information can support early-stage estimation.

Although my research is technical, communication remains an essential part of academic life: discussing ideas, presenting research, reading and writing academic papers, and collaborating with others.

Being a doctoral student has also reminded me that learning is a continuous process. No matter how much experience we have, there is always something new to discover.

## Why I Also Teach English

Alongside my PhD studies, I teach English part-time.

My interest in English teaching is closely connected to my own professional experience. I have seen how important communication skills are in international workplaces, academic environments, and everyday life.

I also understand that learning English can be frustrating.

You may know many English words but still hesitate when speaking. You may understand grammar but find it difficult to express your thoughts naturally. You may pronounce individual words correctly but struggle with rhythm, connected speech, and longer sentences.

These are challenges that many learners experience.

I want my classes to be practical and supportive. Rather than focusing only on memorizing rules, I encourage learners to practise, listen, imitate, express their ideas, and gradually become more confident.

My teaching focuses particularly on:

- **English Pronunciation:** Improving sounds, word stress, sentence rhythm, and intonation.
- **Speaking Fluency:** Developing more natural and confident spoken communication.
- **PTE Core Preparation:** Providing focused practice and feedback for learners preparing for the exam.
- **Practical English Conversation:** Helping learners communicate in everyday, academic, and professional situations.

Every learner has different goals and a different starting point. I believe lessons should reflect those differences.

## My Teaching Philosophy

My professional journey has taught me that knowledge is important, but the ability to communicate that knowledge is equally valuable.

Whether you are discussing a project at work, presenting research at university, preparing for an English exam, or simply having a conversation, the goal is to express yourself clearly and confidently.

I do not believe that learning English is about speaking perfectly from the beginning. It is about practising consistently, learning from mistakes, and becoming a little more confident each time you speak.

**English is not just a subject to study. It is a skill to use — to express ideas, connect with people, and open up new opportunities.**

I am still learning, researching, and growing myself. And I look forward to being part of your English learning journey, too.
