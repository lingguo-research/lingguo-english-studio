# From Zero to B2: My French Learning Journey in Canada

I started learning French in January 2024. At the time, I was starting from zero. In late August 2026, after about two years and eight months of learning, I took the TCF Canada for the first time.

The result was an important milestone in my French-learning journey. It was not the end of the process, but an opportunity to look back at how far I had come.

## January 2024: Starting from zero

When I began, French was a completely new language for me. I had to build my understanding step by step, starting with the basics and gradually becoming more familiar with the language.

Learning French in Quebec gave the process a particular meaning. French was not simply another subject to study; it was also part of the environment around me. However, living in a French-speaking province did not mean that learning the language happened automatically. Progress still required time, attention, and consistent effort.

## Learning over the long term

Looking back, two and a half years can sound like a long time. But language learning is not a straight line. Some things become clearer quickly; others take much longer to settle.

There are periods when progress feels visible and periods when it is difficult to tell how far you have come.

I learned not to judge the whole process by one difficult day, one lesson, or one practice result. The small steps matter. Each time I understood a little more, expressed an idea more clearly, or became more comfortable with French, I was building toward the next stage.

## Preparing for the TCF Canada

Eventually, I needed to turn my general French ability into exam performance.

The TCF Canada assesses four separate skills: listening, reading, speaking, and writing. Preparing for it meant looking at French not only as a language to learn, but also as a language I needed to use under exam conditions.

I took my first TCF Canada exam in late August 2026. It was the first formal assessment of my French-learning journey.

## My first TCF Canada result

The result gave me a clear reference point. More importantly, it allowed me to see what I had achieved after starting from zero in January 2024.

## Every step counts

From January 2024 to August 2026, my French-learning journey took about two years and eight months. It was not a transformation that happened overnight. It was the result of many individual steps accumulated over time.

If you are also learning a language, I hope my experience offers one simple reminder: do not underestimate the value of steady progress. You may not notice the change every day, but the effort is adding up.

My first TCF Canada result is not the end of the journey. It is a milestone—and a reminder that every step counts.
